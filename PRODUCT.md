# Product

## Register

product

## Users

College students, teachers, and coursework evaluators using an anti-fraud media analysis tool in a study, demo, or campus safety context. Students need fast, readable guidance when judging suspicious messages, screenshots, emails, social posts, and URLs. Evaluators need to see that the system is explainable, evidence-backed, and structured enough for experiment review.

## Product Purpose

Anti-Fraud Coach identifies fraud risk in Chinese, English, and mixed-language media content. It combines OCR, risk feature extraction, hybrid RAG retrieval, evidence fusion, reranking, and Qwen reasoning to produce a student-facing risk report, anti-fraud advice, safe rewrite, training question, and structured JSON for evaluation. Success means users can move from suspicious input to clear judgment, evidence, suggested action, and learning feedback without hunting through raw model output.

## Brand Personality

Professional, clear, trustworthy. The interface should feel like an AI risk analysis workstation for campus safety: calm enough for repeated use, technical enough for coursework defense, and direct enough for students under pressure.

## Anti-references

Do not make it look like generic antivirus software, a consumer marketing landing page, a purple AI template, or an over-decorated cyber dashboard. Avoid fake product previews, decorative grid/stripe backgrounds, excessive glow, vague startup copy, and long text walls that hide the user's next action.

## Design Principles

1. Put the workflow first: input, risk judgment, evidence, advice, training, and JSON should form one visible chain.
2. Make evidence inspectable: retrieval scores, matched features, OCR text, and structured fields should be easy to scan and expand.
3. Use calm urgency: high-risk states must be unmistakable without making the whole app feel alarmist.
4. Preserve product familiarity: controls, tabs, upload states, tables, and debug views should behave like dependable app UI.
5. Keep coursework defensibility visible: technical modules such as BM25, BGE-M3, FAISS, URL/domain matching, evidence fusion, reranking, and Qwen reasoning should appear as real system capabilities, not decorative labels.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast for body text, controls, and placeholders. Do not rely on color alone for risk level. Respect reduced-motion preferences and keep motion limited to feedback or state changes. Support bilingual Chinese and English content without cramped labels or clipped text on mobile.
